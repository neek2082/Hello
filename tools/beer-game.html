Beer Game

Four businesses pass beer down a chain: a factory brews it, a distributor and a wholesaler move it, and a shop sells it. Each player runs one business and only sees their own orders. Can your team keep the shelves full without piling up stock?

How to play
  • Each week you see the beer that arrived, the new order you received, and what you shipped.
  • Then you decide how many cases to order from the business above you.
  • Orders take 1 week to reach them, and beer takes 2 weeks to reach you.
  • Every case on your shelves costs $0.50 a week. Every case you still owe costs $1 a week.
  • The game lasts 20 weeks. The team with the lowest total cost wins.

    What happened?

    This is called the bullwhip effect. A small change at the shop (shoppers going from 4 to 8 cases a week) grows bigger at every step up the chain, like a whip cracking. It happens because each business only sees the orders from the one below, and beer takes weeks to arrive, so people over-order to be safe and then get stuck with too much.

    Real companies reduce it by sharing sales figures along the chain, shortening delivery times, and ordering steadily instead of reacting to every change.
