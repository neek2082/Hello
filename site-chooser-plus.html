Why look beyond cost?

The cheapest site today is not always the cheapest site over the years. Things without a price tag now often turn into costs or risks later.

Cost

Rent, transport, electricity and water you pay every year.

Why it matters: it is paid every single year, so a small difference per month adds up to a lot.

Sustainability

How well the site helps the company cut energy use, waste and carbon.

If you ignore it: power bills and carbon taxes keep rising, and big customers may stop buying from suppliers that miss their green targets. In the lesson example, Site 1 pays $336,000 a year for electricity; solar covering just a fifth of that would save about $67,000 every year.

Governance

How stable, clear and fair the local government and its rules are.

If you ignore it: slow permits can delay opening by months, while rules that keep changing or unfair practices bring surprise costs and legal risk.

Labour

Whether enough people with the right skills can work at the site.

If you ignore it: shifts go unfilled, overtime and wages climb, and orders go out late, especially in a warehouse where picking is done by hand.

Key idea: how much each factor should count depends on the company. A low-price business cares most about cost. A company that has promised to cut carbon cannot ignore sustainability. Step 4 helps you decide.
